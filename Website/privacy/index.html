The short version

Rescriva has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It reads, rebuilds and exports your pages on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. If iCloud is on, your library syncs through your own iCloud account, where I can't see it.

The one service I run is the Share Nearby relay. It introduces two of your devices to each other and never sees a file.

Who is responsible

Rescriva is made by Michael Bowman Olay ("I" below). I don't run a server that stores your scans, and I never receive your pages, documents, the text read from them or anything else in your library.

What stays on your device

Rescriva keeps these in its own storage on your device:

  • Your scans: the page images, the text read from them, your fixes, notes, markup, tags and folders.
  • The original photo behind each page, kept so its crop and cleanup can be redone. Originals stay on the device that took them. They aren't synced and aren't included in device backups, and you can remove them in Settings.
  • PDFs you open in Rescriva, with your changes. For now these stay on the device you added them on. They're in that device's backups like other app files, but not in Rescriva's iCloud sync.
  • Your settings.

Reading the page, finding its headings, tables, equations and diagrams, writing the Word, PowerPoint, PDF, Markdown and text files, and reading a scan aloud all happen on your device. Your pages are never uploaded to be read.

Apple Intelligence

On devices with Apple Intelligence, Rescriva asks Apple's on-device language model to pick the likeliest reading of words it isn't sure of, and to tidy up equations it has read. That model runs on your device. Rescriva doesn't send your text to Apple or anyone else for this.

iCloud sync

If iCloud is turned on for Rescriva in your device's settings, your scans and library (folders, tags and notes included) sync through the private database in your own iCloud account, using Apple's CloudKit. That's how they appear on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account.

Apple stores that data under its own privacy policy and your iCloud settings. I can't see it. Original photos and PDFs you've opened in Rescriva aren't synced.

Camera, photos, files and Spotlight

  • Camera. Used only while you're scanning.
  • Photos. You choose pictures in Apple's photo picker. Rescriva gets only the ones you pick and can't see the rest of your library.
  • Files. Rescriva opens only the files you choose, and copies them into its own storage.
  • Spotlight. So you can find a scan from your device's search, each scan's title and text go into the Spotlight index on that device. Deleted scans are taken out.

Share Nearby

Share Nearby sends files between Rescriva and a web browser on another device, through the page at share.rescriva.com. A file travels directly between the two devices over an encrypted WebRTC connection. It's never uploaded to a server.

To introduce the two devices, both connect to a small relay I run at relay.rescriva.com, hosted by Cloudflare. The relay receives:

  • your network's public IP address, so that devices on the same network can find each other;
  • the name and kind of each device (on iPhone and iPad the name is just "iPhone" or "iPad"; on a Mac it's the computer's name);
  • the messages that set up the connection, which include the devices' network addresses.

The relay doesn't receive files, document names or PINs. Those go over the direct, encrypted connection. It uses what it receives only to introduce the devices and doesn't keep it after they disconnect. Cloudflare handles the relay's network traffic under Cloudflare's own privacy policy.

If the relay can't be reached, the devices can pair with a QR code instead, without the relay. Rescriva asks for Local Network access only so Share Nearby can connect to devices on your network.

Safari and links

When you send a web page or PDF to Rescriva from Safari, the extension passes it to the app on your device. If you send a link, Rescriva downloads it directly from that website, or loads the page and saves it as a PDF. The website sees that request like any other visit from your device. Rescriva doesn't use your Safari cookies for it.

What Rescriva doesn't do

  • It doesn't ask for an account, an email address or a login.
  • It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code, and it doesn't follow you across other apps or websites.
  • It doesn't sell, rent or share your information.
  • It doesn't upload your pages to be read, to me or to anyone else.

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may send me crash reports and usage statistics that don't identify you. You can turn that off in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Your choices and deleting data

  • Export any scan as a Word document, a PowerPoint deck, a PDF, Markdown or plain text, and keep it wherever you like.
  • Deleted scans wait in Recently Deleted for 30 days, then they're removed, along with their iCloud copy.
  • Deleting the app removes everything it stored on that device.
  • To remove Rescriva's iCloud data, use your device's iCloud storage settings.

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or move your personal information. Because I don't hold any of it, you can do all of that yourself on your devices. If you have a question, email me.

Children

Rescriva doesn't collect personal information from anyone, children included.

This website

rescriva.com is a static website hosted by GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, has no analytics, and loads nothing from other sites: even its fonts are served from rescriva.com. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses to keep the service secure, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.
